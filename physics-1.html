PART 01

Mechanics

The physics of motion, forces, momentum, energy and rotational effects.

FOUNDATIONS

What is Mechanics?

Mechanics describes the motion of objects and the forces responsible for changing that motion. It provides the mathematical foundation for many areas of physics.

Core idea

Motion is described using quantities such as displacement, velocity and acceleration, while forces explain how and why motion changes.

MECHANICS • 01

Scalars and Vectors

Physical quantities can be classified as scalars or vectors.

Scalars

A scalar has magnitude only.

  • Mass
  • Time
  • Distance
  • Speed
  • Energy
  • Temperature

Vectors

A vector has both magnitude and direction.

  • Displacement
  • Velocity
  • Acceleration
  • Force
  • Momentum
Remember

Distance is a scalar, while displacement is a vector. Speed is a scalar, while velocity is a vector.

MECHANICS • 02

Motion

Displacement

Displacement is the vector change in position of an object.

Velocity

v = Δs / Δt

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement.

Acceleration

a = Δv / Δt

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

Motion Graphs

  • The gradient of a displacement-time graph gives velocity.
  • The gradient of a velocity-time graph gives acceleration.
  • The area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement.

Worked Example

A car increases its velocity from 8 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹ in 4 seconds. Find its acceleration.

a = (20 − 8) / 4

a = 3 m s⁻²

MECHANICS • 03

Constant Acceleration

For motion with constant acceleration, the SUVAT equations can be used.

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t
Symbols

u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, s = displacement, t = time.

MECHANICS • 04

Forces

A force is an interaction that can change the motion or shape of an object.

Newton's Laws

Newton's First Law states that an object remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity unless acted upon by a resultant external force.

Newton's Second Law relates resultant force to the rate of change of momentum. For constant mass:

F = ma

Newton's Third Law states that interacting bodies exert equal and opposite forces on each other.

Weight

W = mg

Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.

Free-Body Diagrams

A free-body diagram shows the forces acting on an object. Always identify the direction and nature of each force.

MECHANICS • 05

Moments

The moment of a force measures its turning effect about a point or axis.

Moment = Force × perpendicular distance

Equilibrium

For an object in rotational equilibrium, the clockwise moments equal the anticlockwise moments.

Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments

Worked Example

A 20 N force acts perpendicular to a beam at a distance of 0.5 m from a pivot. Find its moment.

Moment = 20 × 0.5

Moment = 10 N m

MECHANICS • 06

Momentum

Momentum is a vector quantity associated with the motion of an object.

p = mv

Conservation of Momentum

In an isolated system, total momentum remains constant.

Total momentum before = Total momentum after

Impulse

Impulse = Ft = Δp

The area under a force-time graph represents the impulse.

PART 02

Materials

Explore density, deformation, Hooke's law, stress, strain, Young modulus and elastic energy.

MATERIALS • 01

Density

Density is the mass per unit volume of a material.

ρ = m / V

The SI unit of density is kg m⁻³.

Worked Example

A material has a mass of 0.80 kg and a volume of 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³. Calculate its density.

ρ = 0.80 / (2.0 × 10⁻⁴)

ρ = 4000 kg m⁻³

MATERIALS • 02

Deformation

When a force is applied to an object, it may change shape. This change is called deformation.

Elastic Deformation

An object undergoing elastic deformation returns to its original shape when the deforming force is removed.

Plastic Deformation

Plastic deformation is permanent. The object does not return completely to its original shape when the force is removed.

Elastic limit

Beyond the elastic limit, removing the force does not return the material completely to its original shape.

MATERIALS • 03

Hooke's Law

For a spring or wire operating within its limit of proportionality, extension is proportional to applied force.

F = kx

Here k is the spring constant and x is the extension.

Force-Extension Graph

A straight-line graph through the origin indicates that force is proportional to extension.

Important distinction

The limit of proportionality is where the graph stops being directly proportional. The elastic limit is where permanent deformation begins. They are not necessarily the same point.

MATERIALS • 04

Stress, Strain and Young Modulus

Stress

Stress = F / A

Stress is the force applied per unit cross-sectional area.

Strain

Strain = ΔL / L

Strain is the fractional change in length.

Young Modulus

E = Stress / Strain

Young modulus measures the stiffness of a material.

Worked Example

A wire has cross-sectional area 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ m². A force of 50 N is applied. Calculate the stress.

Stress = 50 / (2.0 × 10⁻⁶)

Stress = 2.5 × 10⁷ Pa

MATERIALS • 05

Elastic Strain Energy

Work done in stretching a spring or wire is stored as elastic strain energy.

E = ½Fx

For a linear force-extension relationship, this corresponds to the area under the force-extension graph.

Graph interpretation

The area under a force-extension graph represents the elastic energy stored.

MATERIALS • 06

Force-Extension and Stress-Strain Behaviour

Force-extension and stress-strain graphs can reveal how a material behaves as it is stretched.

Key Features

  • Region of proportional behaviour
  • Elastic behaviour
  • Plastic deformation
  • Yielding
  • Breaking point

Different materials can behave very differently. A stiff material has a large Young modulus, while a more flexible material has a smaller Young modulus.

PRACTICAL PHYSICS

Experimental Skills

Plan experiments, collect measurements, analyse data and evaluate experimental methods.

⚡

Investigating Free Fall

Measure the motion of an object falling under gravity and use suitable measurements or graphs to determine acceleration due to gravity.

  • Measure time and distance carefully.
  • Repeat measurements to improve reliability.
  • Consider reaction time and systematic errors.
  • Use appropriate graph analysis.
◉

Investigating Viscosity

A falling sphere can be used to investigate viscosity by examining its motion through a fluid.

  • Allow the sphere to reach terminal velocity.
  • Measure distance and time.
  • Control temperature.
  • Repeat measurements.
∆

Determining Young Modulus

Load a wire gradually while measuring its extension and use the data to determine Young modulus.

  • Measure original length accurately.
  • Measure diameter at several positions.
  • Calculate cross-sectional area.
  • Plot an appropriate graph.
Exam tip

When evaluating an experiment, don't simply write “repeat the experiment.” Explain what repeating improves and how the repeated results should be used.

PRACTICAL SKILLS

Measurements and Uncertainties

Experimental measurements are never perfectly exact. Good experimental analysis identifies and considers measurement uncertainty.

Absolute Uncertainty

The uncertainty expressed in the same units as the measured quantity is called absolute uncertainty.

Percentage Uncertainty

Percentage uncertainty = (absolute uncertainty / measured value) × 100

Reducing Uncertainty

  • Repeat measurements.
  • Calculate a mean where appropriate.
  • Use instruments with suitable resolution.
  • Increase the measured quantity where possible.
  • Control relevant variables.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Mixed Unit 1 Example

Question

A 2.0 kg object accelerates from rest to 6.0 m s⁻¹ in 3.0 s. Calculate the resultant force acting on it.

First find acceleration:

a = (6.0 − 0) / 3.0

a = 2.0 m s⁻²

Then use F = ma:

F = 2.0 × 2.0

F = 4.0 N

EXAM PREPARATION

Unit 1 Mixed Practice

Test your understanding before moving on to the next unit.

1. Explain the difference between a scalar and a vector.
Show answer

A scalar has magnitude only, whereas a vector has both magnitude and direction.

2. A car travels from 12 m s⁻¹ to 30 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Calculate its acceleration.
Show answer

a = (30 − 12) / 6

a = 3.0 m s⁻²

3. A 5.0 kg object experiences a resultant force of 20 N. Calculate its acceleration.
Show answer

F = ma

a = 20 / 5.0

a = 4.0 m s⁻²

4. A 15 N force acts 0.40 m from a pivot at right angles. Calculate the moment.
Show answer

Moment = 15 × 0.40

Moment = 6.0 N m

5. State the principle of conservation of momentum.
Show answer

In an isolated system, the total momentum before an interaction equals the total momentum after it.

6. A material has a mass of 0.60 kg and volume 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³. Calculate its density.
Show answer

ρ = m / V

ρ = 0.60 / (3.0 × 10⁻⁴)

ρ = 2.0 × 10³ kg m⁻³

7. State Hooke's law.
Show answer

For a spring or wire within its limit of proportionality, the extension is proportional to the applied force.

8. A wire has a cross-sectional area of 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ m² and is subjected to a force of 80 N. Calculate the stress.
Show answer

Stress = F / A

Stress = 80 / (4.0 × 10⁻⁶)

Stress = 2.0 × 10⁷ Pa

9. Explain the difference between elastic and plastic deformation.
Show answer

Elastic deformation is reversible when the force is removed. Plastic deformation produces a permanent change in shape.

10. Explain why repeated measurements are useful in an experiment.
Show answer

Repeated measurements can help identify random variation and allow a mean value to be calculated, improving the reliability of the result.
