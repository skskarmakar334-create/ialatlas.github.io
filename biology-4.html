02 — Topic 5

Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in organic molecules.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Light energy is required.

Chloroplast structure

Grana

Stacks of thylakoid membranes containing photosynthetic pigments and electron carriers.

Stroma

Fluid region containing enzymes involved in carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle.

Thylakoid membrane

Site of the light-dependent reactions.

Starch grain

Storage form of carbohydrate produced from photosynthetic products.

Light-dependent reactions

  1. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy.
  2. Electrons become excited.
  3. Water undergoes photolysis.
  4. Oxygen is released.
  5. ATP is produced by photophosphorylation.
  6. NADP is reduced to reduced NADP.
Photolysis:

H₂O → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ + ½O₂

Cyclic vs non-cyclic photophosphorylation

Feature Cyclic Non-cyclic
ATP produced Yes Yes
Reduced NADP No Yes
Photolysis No Yes
Oxygen released No Yes

Calvin cycle

The Calvin cycle uses ATP and reduced NADP from the light-dependent reactions to reduce carbon dioxide and produce carbohydrate.

Remember:

Light-dependent reactions → ATP + reduced NADP
Calvin cycle → uses ATP + reduced NADP

03 — Energy Flow

ATP and Energy Transfer

ATP acts as an immediate source of energy for cellular processes.

ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi + energy

Why ATP is useful

  • Releases manageable amounts of energy.
  • Can be rapidly regenerated.
  • Couples energy-releasing reactions to energy-requiring processes.

Respiratory substrates

Carbohydrate

Readily available and commonly used respiratory substrate.

Lipid

High energy value per unit mass.

Protein

Can be used when other substrates are limited.

Energy values

Lipids contain more chemical energy per gram than carbohydrates or proteins.

Energy released by respiration ultimately supports processes such as active transport, synthesis, movement and maintaining body functions.
04 — Ecology

Ecosystems and Energy Flow

Population

Members of the same species living in an area at the same time.

Community

Populations of different species living and interacting in an area.

Habitat

The place where an organism or population normally lives.

Ecosystem

A community and its abiotic environment interacting together.

Energy transfer

Energy enters most ecosystems as light and is converted into chemical energy by producers.

Sun Light
→
Producers Plants / algae
→
Consumers Animals
→
Decomposers Bacteria / fungi

Why energy is lost

  • Respiration releases energy as heat.
  • Not all material is eaten.
  • Some material is indigestible.
  • Energy is lost through waste.

Productivity

GPP = total chemical energy fixed by photosynthesis

NPP = GPP − R

where R = energy lost through respiration.
05 — Ecological Sampling

Sampling Populations

Quadrat sampling

Quadrats are used to investigate organisms that are relatively stationary, such as plants.

Random sampling:

Random coordinates can be generated so that every area has an appropriate chance of being selected.

Transects

Transects can be used to investigate how the distribution of organisms changes along an environmental gradient.

Line transect

Records organisms touching or occurring along a line.

Belt transect

Uses a strip of defined width to sample organisms across a gradient.

Sampling reliability

  • Increase sample size.
  • Use random sampling where appropriate.
  • Repeat sampling.
  • Sample different areas.
Population estimate = mean number per quadrat × total area ÷ quadrat area
06 — Environment

Climate Change

Earth's climate changes naturally, but human activities have significantly altered atmospheric concentrations of greenhouse gases.

Greenhouse gases

  • Carbon dioxide
  • Methane
  • Nitrous oxide

Major human sources

  • Burning fossil fuels
  • Deforestation
  • Agriculture
  • Industrial activity
Greenhouse effect: Atmospheric gases absorb and re-radiate infrared radiation, reducing the rate at which heat escapes to space.

Evidence

Evidence for climate change can include long-term temperature records, atmospheric carbon dioxide measurements, ice-core data, sea-level changes and changes in species distributions.

Correlation between two variables does not by itself establish causation. Scientists consider multiple lines of evidence and alternative explanations.
07 — Evolution

Natural Selection and Speciation

Natural selection

  1. Individuals in a population show variation.
  2. Some variation is heritable.
  3. There is competition for resources.
  4. Individuals with advantageous characteristics are more likely to survive and reproduce.
  5. Their alleles become more common in the population over generations.
Evolution can be described as a change in allele frequencies in a population over generations.

Speciation

Speciation occurs when populations become reproductively isolated and diverge genetically.

Allopatric speciation

Geographic separation prevents gene flow between populations.

Sympatric speciation

Reproductive isolation develops without geographic separation.

Core chain:

Variation → selection pressure → differential reproductive success → allele-frequency change → divergence → reproductive isolation

08 — Topic 6

Microbiology and Microbial Growth

Aseptic technique

Aseptic technique reduces contamination of cultures and prevents microorganisms from being transferred between cultures, people and the environment.

  • Sterilise equipment where appropriate.
  • Disinfect work surfaces.
  • Minimise exposure of cultures.
  • Use sterile apparatus.
  • Follow appropriate laboratory safety procedures.

Microbial growth

Lag phase

Cells adapt to the new environment.

Log phase

Rapid cell division and exponential growth.

Stationary phase

Growth rate slows as resources become limiting.

Factors affecting growth

  • Temperature
  • pH
  • Nutrient availability
  • Oxygen availability
  • Waste-product accumulation
09 — Pathogens

Pathogens and Infectious Disease

Bacteria

Prokaryotic organisms. Some cause disease by producing toxins or damaging tissues.

Viruses

Acellular infectious particles that replicate inside host cells.

Fungi

Eukaryotic organisms that can cause infections in humans.

Protoctists

Some protoctists are pathogenic and can cause serious disease.

Transmission

  • Direct contact
  • Droplets
  • Contaminated food or water
  • Vectors
  • Contaminated surfaces
Preventing transmission can involve hygiene, sanitation, vaccination, isolation and vector control.
10 — Immunity

The Immune Response

Non-specific defenses

  • Skin
  • Mucus
  • Ciliated epithelial cells
  • Stomach acid
  • Lysozyme
  • Phagocytic cells

Phagocytosis

  1. Phagocyte detects the pathogen.
  2. Pathogen is engulfed.
  3. A phagosome forms.
  4. Lysosomes fuse with the vesicle.
  5. Digestive enzymes destroy the pathogen.

Specific immunity

Lymphocytes recognise specific antigens and produce an immune response.

B lymphocytes

Produce antibodies and form memory cells.

T lymphocytes

Participate in cell-mediated immune responses and help coordinate immunity.

Antibodies

Antibodies have specific binding sites that recognise complementary antigens.

Antigen: A molecule or structure recognised by the immune system and capable of triggering a specific immune response.

Vaccination

Vaccination exposes the immune system to an antigen so that memory cells can develop. On later exposure to the pathogen, the secondary immune response is faster and stronger.

Primary response: Slower and smaller.

Secondary response: Faster and larger due to memory cells.
11 — Antibiotics

Antibiotics and Resistance

Antibiotics target bacterial structures or processes and therefore do not directly treat viral infections.

Antibiotic resistance

  1. Random genetic variation produces a resistant bacterium.
  2. Antibiotic exposure kills susceptible bacteria.
  3. Resistant bacteria survive.
  4. Resistant bacteria reproduce.
  5. The resistance allele becomes more common.
Selection pressure: Antibiotic treatment

Selected population: Resistant bacteria

Reducing resistance

  • Use antibiotics only when appropriate.
  • Avoid unnecessary prescriptions.
  • Use appropriate treatment regimes.
  • Develop new antimicrobial drugs.
  • Reduce transmission of resistant organisms.
12 — Decomposition

Microorganisms and Nutrient Cycling

Microorganisms play major roles as decomposers, breaking down organic material and returning nutrients to ecosystems.

Decomposition

  1. Microorganisms release extracellular enzymes.
  2. Large molecules are hydrolysed into smaller soluble molecules.
  3. Soluble molecules are absorbed.
  4. Microorganisms respire and grow.
  5. Carbon and mineral ions are returned to the environment.
Temperature, oxygen availability, moisture and pH can influence decomposition.
13 — Forensics

DNA Analysis and Forensic Biology

DNA profiling

DNA profiling can compare DNA samples by analysing differences in DNA fragments.

Gel electrophoresis

DNA fragments can be separated according to size using an electric field through a gel.

Basic sequence:

DNA sample → fragments → electrical field → separation → banding pattern → comparison

Why do smaller fragments move further?

Smaller DNA fragments move more easily through the pores in the gel and therefore travel further in the same period of time.

A DNA match indicates that samples have compatible DNA profiles; interpretation of forensic evidence also requires appropriate statistical and contextual analysis.

16 — Exam Practice

Unit 4 Practice

1. State two products of the light-dependent reactions that are used in the Calvin cycle.
Show answer

ATP and reduced NADP.

2. Explain why oxygen is produced during photosynthesis.
Show answer

Water undergoes photolysis during the light-dependent reactions, producing oxygen as a product.

3. Why is energy lost between trophic levels?
Show answer

Energy is lost through respiration and released as heat. Some biomass is also uneaten or not assimilated.

4. Why are quadrats useful when studying plants?
Show answer

Plants are generally stationary, so quadrats provide a defined area in which abundance or distribution can be measured.

5. Explain how antibiotic resistance can increase in a bacterial population.
Show answer

Genetic variation produces resistant bacteria. Antibiotic treatment kills susceptible bacteria, while resistant bacteria survive and reproduce. Their resistance alleles therefore become more common in the population.

6. What is the role of memory cells after vaccination?
Show answer

Memory cells remain after the initial immune response and allow a faster and stronger response if the same antigen is encountered again.

7. Why do smaller DNA fragments travel further through a gel?
Show answer

Smaller fragments pass more easily through the pores in the gel and therefore migrate further in the same time.

8. Give two factors that can affect decomposition.
Show answer

Temperature, oxygen availability, moisture and pH are acceptable examples.

17 — Exam Strategy

How to Attack a Unit 4 Question

1 — Identify the biological process

Photosynthesis? Ecology? Immunity? Microbiology? Evolution?

2 — Identify the command word

State, describe, explain, calculate, compare, evaluate or suggest.

3 — Use precise biological terminology

Avoid vague phrases such as "energy disappears." Say that energy is transferred and eventually dissipated as heat.

4 — Link cause to consequence

Especially in six-mark explanations, connect each biological step logically.

5 — Use data

When a graph or table is provided, quote relevant values and describe patterns.
