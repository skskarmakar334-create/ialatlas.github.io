MECHANICS 1 • TOPIC 01

Mathematical Models in Mechanics

Learn how mechanics problems are simplified into mathematical models.

LESSON 01

What is a Mathematical Model?

A mathematical model is a simplified representation of a real-life situation using mathematics.

In mechanics, real objects and situations can be complicated. A model removes details that are not important to the problem so that mathematical methods can be used.

Definition

A mathematical model represents a physical situation using assumptions and mathematical relationships.

Why do we use models?

  • Real physical situations can be complicated.
  • Ignoring unnecessary details makes calculations possible.
  • The model allows us to predict motion and forces.
  • The model can then be compared with the real situation.

LESSON 02

Particles

A particle is an object whose size and shape are ignored. Its entire mass is treated as though it acts at a single point.

Particle model

The dimensions and shape of the object are ignored, while its mass is considered concentrated at one point.

Example

A car travels along a straight road and is modelled as a particle.

What is being ignored?

The size and shape of the car are ignored.

LESSON 03

Strings

Light string

A string is described as light when its mass is considered negligible.

Light string = negligible mass

Inextensible string

An inextensible string does not stretch. Therefore, its length remains constant while it is taut.

Inextensible string = constant length

Example

Two particles are connected by a light, inextensible string.

The string has negligible mass and does not stretch.

LESSON 04

Smooth and Rough Surfaces

Smooth surface

A smooth surface means that friction is ignored.

Smooth surface → no frictional force

Rough surface

A rough surface allows friction to act between the object and the surface.

Rough surface → friction may act

Example

A particle moves along a smooth horizontal surface.

Since the surface is smooth, friction is ignored.

LESSON 05

Uniform Objects

An object is uniform when its mass is distributed evenly throughout the object.

Uniform object → mass is distributed evenly.

This assumption becomes particularly important later when studying centres of mass and rigid bodies.

LESSON 06

Rigid Bodies

A rigid body is an object whose shape is assumed not to change.

Rigid body → shape does not change.

This allows us to study forces and turning effects without considering deformation of the object.

LESSON 07

Choosing the Correct Model

In an exam question, look carefully at the words used to describe the situation.

  • Particle → ignore size and shape.
  • Light string → ignore string mass.
  • Inextensible → string does not stretch.
  • Smooth → ignore friction.
  • Rough → friction may act.
  • Uniform → mass is evenly distributed.
  • Rigid → shape does not change.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Putting the Ideas Together

A particle of mass 4 kg is attached to another particle by a light, inextensible string. The string passes over a smooth pulley.

Identify the modelling assumptions.

Particle: The sizes and shapes of the objects are ignored.

Light string: The mass of the string is negligible.

Inextensible: The string does not stretch.

Smooth pulley: Friction at the pulley is ignored.

EXAM SKILLS

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1:

Thinking "smooth" means the object moves faster. It actually means friction is ignored.

Mistake 2:

Thinking "light" means the string has no tension. A light string can still have tension.

Mistake 3:

Confusing "light" with "inextensible". They describe different properties.
