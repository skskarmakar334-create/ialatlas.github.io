Mathematical Models in Mechanics

Learn how real physical situations are simplified into mathematical models that can be analysed using mechanics.

What is a Mathematical Model?

A mathematical model is a simplified representation of a real physical situation.

In mechanics, we use models to represent objects, forces and motion in a way that allows us to solve problems mathematically.

Key idea: A model simplifies reality while keeping the important features needed to solve the problem.

Common Modelling Assumptions

  • Particle: the size and shape of an object are ignored.
  • Light object: its mass is considered negligible.
  • Light string: the mass of the string is ignored.
  • Inextensible string: the string does not stretch.
  • Smooth surface: friction is ignored.
  • Rough surface: friction may act between the surfaces.
  • Uniform body: mass is distributed evenly.
  • Rigid body: the body does not change shape.

Understanding the Assumptions

Example 1 — Smooth surface

A particle moves along a smooth horizontal surface.

What does "smooth" mean?

Friction is ignored.

Example 2 — Light string

Two particles are connected by a light string.

What does "light" mean?

The mass of the string is considered negligible.

Example 3 — Inextensible string

Two particles are connected by an inextensible string.

What does "inextensible" mean?

The string does not stretch, so its length remains constant.

Practice Questions

Question 1

A car is modelled as a particle. What features of the car are being ignored?

Show answer

The size and shape of the car are ignored.

Question 2

A particle moves along a smooth surface. What force is being ignored?

Show answer

Friction is being ignored.

Question 3

What does it mean when a string is described as inextensible?

Show answer

The string does not stretch, so its length remains constant.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting that a smooth surface means no friction.
  • Confusing a light string with an inextensible string.
  • Forgetting that a particle model ignores size and shape.
  • Ignoring modelling assumptions stated in the question.

Exam Tips

  • Always identify the modelling assumptions before starting.
  • Translate words such as smooth, light and inextensible into their mathematical consequences.
  • When asked to explain an assumption, state precisely what physical effect is being ignored.
