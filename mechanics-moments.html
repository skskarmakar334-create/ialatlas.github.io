LESSON 01

What is a Moment?

A moment measures the turning effect of a force about a point.

Key idea

A force can make an object rotate about a point. This turning effect is called a moment.

LESSON 02

Moment of a Force

The moment of a force about a point is calculated by multiplying the force by the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force.

Moment = Force × perpendicular distance

The unit of moment is the newton metre (N m).

LESSON 03

Clockwise and Anticlockwise Moments

A force can produce either a clockwise or anticlockwise turning effect.

Remember:

Always identify the direction in which the force tends to rotate the object.

  • Clockwise moment → turning clockwise.
  • Anticlockwise moment → turning anticlockwise.

LESSON 04

Equilibrium of Moments

When a body is in rotational equilibrium, the total clockwise moment equals the total anticlockwise moment.

Total clockwise moments = Total anticlockwise moments

This is known as the principle of moments.

LESSON 05

Choosing a Pivot

The point about which moments are calculated is called the pivot.

Useful strategy

Choose a pivot that makes one or more unknown forces have zero moment.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Calculating a Moment

Example

A force of 20 N acts perpendicular to a beam at a distance of 3 m from a pivot. Find the moment about the pivot.

Moment = Force × perpendicular distance

Moment = 20 × 3

Moment = 60 N m

WORKED EXAMPLE

Principle of Moments

Example

A beam is in equilibrium. A clockwise moment of 40 N m acts about a pivot. What must the total anticlockwise moment be?

In equilibrium:

Clockwise moments = Anticlockwise moments

Therefore:

Anticlockwise moment = 40 N m
