MECHANICS 1 • TOPIC 04

Dynamics of a Particle

Understand how forces affect the motion of particles using Newton's laws of motion.

LESSON 01

What is Dynamics?

Dynamics is the study of how forces affect the motion of particles.

Key idea

Kinematics describes motion, while dynamics explains how forces cause changes in motion.

LESSON 02

Newton's Second Law

The most important equation in M1 dynamics is Newton's Second Law.

F = ma

Here F is the resultant force, m is the mass of the particle and a is its acceleration.

  • F is measured in newtons (N).
  • m is measured in kilograms (kg).
  • a is measured in metres per second squared (m/s²).

LESSON 03

Resultant Force

The resultant force is the single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on a particle.

Important:

Always consider the direction of each force before calculating the resultant force.

If forces act in opposite directions, subtract their magnitudes.

LESSON 04

Weight

The weight of a particle is the force caused by gravity.

W = mg

On Earth, the acceleration due to gravity is commonly taken as:

g = 9.8 m/s²

unless the question specifies another value.

LESSON 05

Newton's First Law

Newton's First Law states that a particle remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity unless acted upon by a resultant force.

Equilibrium

If the resultant force is zero, the acceleration is zero.

LESSON 06

Newton's Third Law

When two objects interact, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other.

Remember:

The two forces act on different bodies.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Using F = ma

Example

A particle of mass 4 kg is acted on by a resultant force of 20 N. Find its acceleration.

Use:

F = ma

20 = 4a

a = 20 ÷ 4

a = 5 m/s²

PRACTICE

Test Yourself

1. State Newton's Second Law.
Show answer

The resultant force on a particle equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration: F = ma.

2. Find the weight of a 5 kg particle using g = 9.8 m/s².
Show answer

W = mg = 5 × 9.8

W = 49 N

3. A 6 kg particle has a resultant force of 30 N. Find its acceleration.
Show answer

F = ma

30 = 6a

a = 5 m/s²

4. What happens when the resultant force is zero?
Show answer

The acceleration is zero, so the particle remains at rest or moves with constant velocity.

QUICK REVIEW

Remember These

F = ma
W = mg
Resultant force = mass × acceleration
Resultant force = 0 → acceleration = 0
