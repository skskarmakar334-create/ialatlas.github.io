LESSON 01

Revisiting Kinematics

Kinematics describes the motion of particles without considering the forces causing that motion.

Key quantities
  • Displacement — measured in metres (m)
  • Velocity — measured in metres per second (m s⁻¹)
  • Acceleration — measured in metres per second squared (m s⁻²)
  • Time — measured in seconds (s)

LESSON 02

Variable Acceleration

In more advanced mechanics problems, acceleration does not always remain constant.

When acceleration varies with time, velocity can be found by integrating acceleration with respect to time.

v = ∫a dt

Similarly, displacement can be found by integrating velocity.

s = ∫v dt

LESSON 03

Velocity as a Function of Time

If velocity is given as a function of time, displacement can be found by integrating the velocity function.

s = ∫v(t) dt

Example

A particle has velocity

v = 3t²

Find its displacement from t = 0 to t = 2.

s = ∫₀² 3t² dt

s = [t³]₀²

s = 8 m

LESSON 04

Acceleration as a Function of Time

If acceleration is given as a function of time, integrate it to obtain velocity.

v = ∫a(t) dt

Remember that an initial velocity may be required to determine the constant of integration.

LESSON 05

Using Initial Conditions

Initial conditions allow us to determine unknown constants when integrating.

Example:

If a particle has velocity 5 m s⁻¹ when t = 0, substitute t = 0 and v = 5 into the velocity equation to determine the constant.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Finding Velocity

Example

A particle has acceleration

a = 6t

Its initial velocity is 4 m s⁻¹. Find its velocity at time t.

v = ∫6t dt

v = 3t² + C

At t = 0, v = 4.

Therefore C = 4.

v = 3t² + 4
