Trigonometry

Edexcel International A Level Mathematics

1. Basic Trigonometric Ratios

In a right-angled triangle, the three main trigonometric ratios are sine, cosine and tangent.

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite / adjacent

A useful memory aid is:

SOH — CAH — TOA

Worked Example

A right-angled triangle has opposite side 6 and hypotenuse 10. Find θ.

sin θ = 6/10
sin θ = 0.6
θ = sin⁻¹(0.6)
θ ≈ 36.87°

2. Exact Trigonometric Values

You should know the exact values of sine, cosine and tangent for the standard angles.

sin 0° = 0
sin 30° = 1/2
sin 45° = √2/2
sin 60° = √3/2
sin 90° = 1
cos 0° = 1
cos 30° = √3/2
cos 45° = √2/2
cos 60° = 1/2
cos 90° = 0
tan 0° = 0
tan 30° = √3/3
tan 45° = 1
tan 60° = √3

3. Trigonometric Identities

An identity is an equation that is true for all values for which both sides are defined.

Fundamental Identity

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Other Useful Identities

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
sec²θ = 1 + tan²θ
cosec²θ = 1 + cot²θ

Worked Example

Given sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find cos θ.

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
(3/5)² + cos²θ = 1
9/25 + cos²θ = 1
cos²θ = 16/25
cos θ = 4/5

4. Trigonometric Equations

When solving trigonometric equations, always consider the complete interval given in the question.

Worked Example

Solve:

sin θ = 1/2

for 0° ≤ θ ≤ 360°.

θ = 30°

Sine is also positive in the second quadrant.

θ = 180° − 30°
θ = 150°
θ = 30°, 150°

5. The Sine Rule

The sine rule is useful when you know a side and its opposite angle, together with another side or angle.

a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C

Worked Example

Suppose:

A = 30°, B = 60°, a = 5

Find b.

5/sin30° = b/sin60°
b = 5(sin60°) / sin30°
b = 5√3

6. The Cosine Rule

The cosine rule can be used when two sides and the included angle are known, or when all three sides are known.

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A

Worked Example

If b = 5, c = 7 and A = 60°, find a.

a² = 5² + 7² − 2(5)(7)cos60°
a² = 25 + 49 − 35
a² = 39
a = √39

7. Area of a Triangle

When two sides and the included angle are known, the area can be calculated using:

Area = 1/2 ab sin C

Worked Example

Find the area of a triangle with sides 8 and 5 and included angle 30°.

Area = 1/2(8)(5)sin30°
= 20 × 1/2
= 10

8. Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions

The reciprocal trigonometric functions are secant, cosecant and cotangent.

sec θ = 1 / cos θ
cosec θ = 1 / sin θ
cot θ = 1 / tan θ

Useful Relationships

sec²θ = 1 + tan²θ
cosec²θ = 1 + cot²θ

9. CAST Diagram and Quadrants

The CAST rule helps determine where sine, cosine and tangent are positive.

C — cosine positive
A — all positive
S — sine positive
T — tangent positive

Therefore:

Quadrant I → all positive
Quadrant II → sine positive
Quadrant III → tangent positive
Quadrant IV → cosine positive

Example

If cos θ = −1/2 for 0° ≤ θ ≤ 360°, cosine is negative in Quadrants II and III.

θ = 120°, 240°

10. Compound Angles

The compound-angle identities allow expressions involving sums and differences of angles to be expanded.

sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B
cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B) / (1 ∓ tan A tan B)

Worked Example

Expand sin(A + B).

sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B

11. Double-Angle Identities

Double-angle identities are obtained by setting A = B in the compound-angle formulas.

sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A
cos 2A = 2cos²A − 1
cos 2A = 1 − 2sin²A
tan 2A = 2tan A / (1 − tan²A)

12. Trigonometric Graphs

The three basic trigonometric graphs are sine, cosine and tangent.

y = sin x

Range: −1 ≤ sin x ≤ 1
Period: 360° or 2π radians

y = cos x

Range: −1 ≤ cos x ≤ 1
Period: 360° or 2π radians

y = tan x

Period: 180° or π radians

Tangent has vertical asymptotes where cos x = 0.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. Calculator in the wrong mode

Check whether your calculator is in degrees or radians before solving the question.

2. Missing solutions

Always check every quadrant in the specified interval.

3. Choosing the wrong rule

Decide whether the Sine Rule, Cosine Rule or another trigonometric relationship is appropriate before calculating.

4. Using the wrong angle in the area formula

The formula 1/2ab sin C requires the angle between the two known sides.

5. Rounding too early

Keep accurate values throughout your working and round only at the end when required.

6. Forgetting the CAST rule

When solving equations, identify the quadrants in which the trigonometric function has the required sign.

🎯 Exam Tips

1. Check calculator mode

Make sure you are using degrees or radians according to the question.

2. Write the formula first

Writing the correct relationship before substituting values reduces errors.

3. Use the full interval

For trigonometric equations, do not stop after finding the principal value.

4. Learn the exact values

Exact values are essential for many Edexcel questions and often allow you to avoid unnecessary calculator work.

5. Keep exact answers

If an exact answer is required, leave surds and fractions in exact form.

6. Draw a diagram when necessary

A clear triangle or sketch can make the correct relationship much easier to identify.
