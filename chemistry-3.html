TOPIC 02

Accuracy, Precision & Uncertainty

Accuracy

Accuracy describes how close a measurement is to the accepted or true value.

Precision

Precision describes how closely repeated measurements agree with one another.

Random Error

Causes readings to vary unpredictably. Repeating measurements and calculating a mean can reduce its effect.

Systematic Error

Produces a consistent shift in measurements and is not removed simply by repeating the experiment.

REMEMBER

Repeats can reduce the effect of random error, but they do not eliminate systematic error.

TOPIC 03

Independent

The variable deliberately changed by the experimenter.

02

Dependent

The variable measured as a result of changing the independent variable.

03

Variables kept constant so the investigation is a fair test.

Planning an Investigation

  • Identify the independent variable.
  • Identify the dependent variable.
  • Control relevant variables.
  • Use an appropriate range of values.
  • Repeat measurements where appropriate.
  • Record results clearly.

TOPIC 04

Safety & Risk Assessment

Practical chemistry requires hazards to be identified and appropriate precautions to be considered.

Hazard

A property of a substance or procedure that can cause harm.

Risk

The likelihood that a hazard will actually cause harm under the circumstances.

Risk Reduction

  • Use appropriate personal protective equipment.
  • Use suitable quantities.
  • Use appropriate ventilation where necessary.
  • Follow the correct experimental procedure.
  • Dispose of chemicals appropriately.

TOPIC 05

Titration

Titration is used to determine the concentration of a solution by reacting it with a solution of known concentration.

Typical Apparatus

🔬
Burette
💧
Pipette
⚗️
Conical flask
🎨
Indicator

Burette Readings

The initial and final burette readings are recorded and the titre is calculated from their difference.

Titre = Final reading − Initial reading

Concordant Results

Repeated titres should be sufficiently close together before a mean titre is calculated.

EXAM TIP

Always pay attention to the burette scale and record readings to the appropriate precision.

TOPIC 07

Calorimetry

Calorimetry can be used to investigate energy changes associated with chemical reactions.

q = mcΔT

where q is the energy transferred, m is the mass, c is the specific heat capacity and ΔT is the temperature change.

Energy transfer ACTIVE

Experimental Considerations

  • Measure temperature accurately.
  • Use an appropriate volume of solution.
  • Reduce heat loss to the surroundings.
  • Stir the solution where appropriate.
  • Record the maximum or minimum temperature reached.

TOPIC 08

Investigating Reaction Rates

Practical investigations can be used to determine how changing conditions affects the rate of a reaction.

Possible Measurements

  • Volume of gas produced.
  • Mass lost.
  • Time taken for a visible change.
  • Concentration change.
Rate ∝ 1 / time

Where the same endpoint is used, comparing reciprocal times can provide a simple measure of relative rate.

TOPIC 09

Qualitative Observations & Chemical Tests

Practical questions may require accurate descriptions of observations rather than explanations of what is happening at particle level.

Good Observations

  • Colour changes.
  • Formation of a precipitate.
  • Dissolving of a solid.
  • Gas production.
  • Temperature changes.
  • Changes in appearance.
OBSERVATION VS INFERENCE

An observation describes what is seen or measured. An inference explains what that observation suggests.

Mean

TOPIC 11

Graphs & Data Analysis

Graphs allow relationships between variables to be investigated visually.

Good Graph Practice

  • Put the independent variable on the x-axis.
  • Put the dependent variable on the y-axis.
  • Label both axes with quantities and units.
  • Use an appropriate scale.
  • Plot points accurately.
  • Draw an appropriate line or curve of best fit.
Independent variable Dependent variable

Gradient

The gradient can be calculated using two well-separated points on a line of best fit.

Gradient = Change in y / Change in x

TOPIC 12

Errors, Limitations & Improvements

Identifying Problems

Experimental results may be affected by heat loss, incomplete reactions, inaccurate measurements, contamination or limitations of apparatus.

Suggesting Improvements

  • Use more precise measuring equipment.
  • Repeat measurements and calculate a mean.
  • Use insulation to reduce heat loss.
  • Increase the number of readings.
  • Use a wider range of values where appropriate.
  • Control variables more carefully.
IMPORTANT

An improvement should directly address the identified limitation. Do not give a generic improvement without explaining what problem it solves.

PRACTICE

Unit 3 Mixed Practice

1. Explain why a burette is more suitable than a measuring cylinder for measuring a titre.
Show answer

A burette allows a solution to be delivered and measured more precisely, making it suitable for determining an accurate titre.

2. Explain the difference between random and systematic error.
Show answer

Random errors cause measurements to vary unpredictably, whereas systematic errors cause a consistent shift in measurements.

3. A solution contains 0.025 mol of solute in 0.500 dm³. Calculate its concentration.
Show answer

c = n / V

c = 0.025 / 0.500

c = 0.050 mol dm⁻³

4. State two ways of reducing heat loss during a calorimetry experiment.
Show answer

Insulate the apparatus and use a lid where appropriate. These reduce energy transfer to the surroundings.

5. Why are repeated measurements useful?
Show answer

Repeats can reveal anomalous results and allow a mean value to be calculated, reducing the effect of random variation.

6. State the independent variable when investigating how temperature affects reaction rate.
Show answer

Temperature.

7. Explain why observations should be recorded immediately during an experiment.
Show answer

Recording observations immediately reduces the risk of forgetting or inaccurately recalling what was observed.

8. State two features of a well-presented results table.
Show answer

Clear headings and appropriate units should be included. Values should also be recorded consistently.
