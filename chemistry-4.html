Chemistry 4

General Principles of Chemistry I — Rates, Equilibria and Further Organic Chemistry.

TOPIC 01

How Fast? — Rates of Reaction

Reaction kinetics describes how quickly chemical reactions occur and how the rate changes when conditions are altered.

Rate of Reaction

Rate can be measured using the change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.

Rate = change in concentration / time

Initial Rate

The initial rate is the rate of reaction at the beginning of a reaction. It can be determined from the gradient of a tangent to a concentration-time graph at t = 0.

Factors Affecting Rate

  • Concentration of reactants
  • Pressure of gaseous reactants
  • Temperature
  • Surface area
  • Presence of a catalyst

Collision Theory

Particles must collide with sufficient energy and suitable orientation for a successful reaction to occur.

Activation Energy

Activation energy:

The minimum energy required for particles to react successfully during a collision.

TOPIC 02

Rate Equations

Experimental data can be used to determine how the concentrations of reactants affect the rate.

Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ

Order of Reaction

The order with respect to a reactant is the power of its concentration in the experimentally determined rate equation.

  • Zero order — rate is independent of concentration.
  • First order — rate is proportional to concentration.
  • Second order — rate is proportional to concentration squared.

Overall Order

The overall order is the sum of the individual orders in the rate equation.

Overall order = m + n

Rate Constant

The value and units of the rate constant depend on the overall order of the reaction.

TOPIC 03

Temperature and the Arrhenius Equation

Increasing temperature generally increases the rate of reaction because a greater proportion of particles have energy greater than the activation energy.

k = Ae−Eₐ/RT

Arrhenius Equation

  • k = rate constant
  • A = pre-exponential factor
  • Eₐ = activation energy
  • R = gas constant
  • T = temperature in kelvin

Arrhenius Plots

ln k = ln A − Eₐ / RT

A graph of ln k against 1/T can be used to determine the activation energy from its gradient.

TOPIC 04

How Far? — Entropy

Entropy is a measure of the disorder or dispersal of energy and particles in a system.

Entropy Change

ΔS = ΣS(products) − ΣS(reactants)

Entropy generally increases when particles become more dispersed, particularly when a solid or liquid forms a gas.

Total Entropy Change

ΔStotal = ΔSsystem + ΔSsurroundings
Spontaneous change:

A reaction is thermodynamically feasible when the total entropy change is positive.

TOPIC 05

Equilibria

Dynamic equilibrium occurs when the forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates in a closed system.

Equilibrium Constant

Kc = [products]coefficients / [reactants]coefficients

The equilibrium constant provides information about the position of equilibrium at a particular temperature.

Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change, the equilibrium position shifts in the direction that opposes the change.

  • Changing concentration
  • Changing pressure
  • Changing temperature

Industrial Equilibria

Industrial processes use equilibrium principles to balance yield, rate, cost and safety.

TOPIC 06

Acid-Base Equilibria

Acids and bases can be treated as equilibrium systems, particularly when considering weak acids and weak bases.

Weak Acids

HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻

Acid Dissociation Constant

Ka = [H⁺][A⁻] / [HA]

pH

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]

Buffers

Buffer solutions resist changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added.

Titration Curves

Titration curves show how pH changes as acid or alkali is added. They can be used to identify equivalence points and appropriate indicators.

TOPIC 07

Carbonyl Compounds

Aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl functional group, C=O.

Aldehydes

Aldehydes contain the functional group −CHO and can be oxidised to carboxylic acids.

Ketones

Ketones contain a carbonyl group bonded to two carbon groups and are generally resistant to further oxidation.

Nucleophilic Addition

Nucleophiles can attack the electrophilic carbon atom of the carbonyl group.

Reaction with Hydrogen Cyanide

Aldehydes and ketones can react with hydrogen cyanide to form hydroxynitriles.

Key idea:

The carbonyl carbon is electron deficient because oxygen is more electronegative than carbon.

TOPIC 08

Carboxylic Acids and Their Derivatives

Carboxylic acids contain the functional group −COOH and undergo a range of reactions useful in organic synthesis.

Properties

  • Carboxylic acids are weak acids.
  • They can form hydrogen bonds.
  • They react with bases to form salts.
  • They can react with alcohols to form esters.

Esters

Esters can be formed by the reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.

Carboxylic acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water

Hydrolysis

Esters can be hydrolysed under acidic or alkaline conditions.

Acyl Derivatives

The chemistry of acyl compounds involves nucleophilic substitution and is important in organic synthesis.

TOPIC 09

Organic Synthesis and Analysis

Organic synthesis involves converting one organic compound into another using appropriate reagents and conditions.

Reaction Pathways

  • Identify the functional group being changed.
  • Select an appropriate reagent.
  • State suitable reaction conditions.
  • Identify the organic product.
  • Balance the equation where required.

NMR Spectroscopy

Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy provides information about the chemical environment of nuclei within molecules.

  • Number of signals
  • Chemical shift
  • Integration
  • Splitting patterns

PRACTICE

Unit 4 Mixed Practice

1. What is meant by the order of a reaction with respect to a reactant?
Show answer

It is the power to which the concentration of that reactant is raised in the experimentally determined rate equation.

2. State two factors that can increase the rate of a reaction.
Show answer

Increasing temperature and increasing concentration are two possible factors.

3. What does a positive total entropy change indicate?
Show answer

A positive total entropy change indicates that the reaction is thermodynamically feasible.

4. State Le Chatelier's principle.
Show answer

When a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change, the equilibrium position shifts in the direction that opposes the change.

5. What is the functional group present in aldehydes?
Show answer

The aldehyde functional group is −CHO.

6. What type of reaction occurs when an aldehyde reacts with hydrogen cyanide?
Show answer

It is a nucleophilic addition reaction.
