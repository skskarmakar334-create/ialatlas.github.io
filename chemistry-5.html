TOPIC 01 • REDOX EQUILIBRIA

Redox Equilibria

Redox reactions involve the transfer of electrons between chemical species. Oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.

Oxidation and Reduction

  • Oxidation involves loss of electrons.
  • Reduction involves gain of electrons.
  • An oxidising agent accepts electrons.
  • A reducing agent donates electrons.
OIL RIG

Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.

Standard Electrode Potentials

Standard electrode potentials allow the relative tendency of chemical species to be reduced to be compared.

E°cell = E°reduction − E°oxidation

A positive E°cell indicates thermodynamic feasibility under standard conditions.

TOPIC 02 • TRANSITION METALS

Transition Metals

Transition metals are important because of their variable oxidation states, complex ions, coloured compounds and catalytic properties.

Definition

A transition element is an element whose atom has a partially filled d subshell, or which forms at least one ion with a partially filled d subshell.

Variable Oxidation States

Transition metals can form ions with different oxidation states because the 3d and 4s electrons have similar energies.

Complex Ions

A complex ion contains a central metal ion surrounded by ligands which donate lone pairs of electrons to the metal.

Ligands

  • Monodentate ligands form one coordinate bond.
  • Bidentate ligands form two coordinate bonds.
  • Multidentate ligands can form several coordinate bonds.

TOPIC 03 • COLOUR & LIGAND EXCHANGE

Colours and Ligand Exchange

Many transition-metal complexes are coloured because electrons can absorb visible radiation and undergo transitions between split d-orbitals.

Why Are Complexes Coloured?

The colour observed is complementary to the wavelengths of visible light absorbed by the complex.

Transition-Metal Colour Field
Complex A Absorption → colour
Complex B Different ligand
Complex C Different ΔE
Complex D New absorption

Ligand Substitution

Ligand exchange occurs when one ligand is replaced by another ligand in a complex ion. This can change the coordination environment and colour.

Precipitation Reactions

Transition-metal ions can form insoluble hydroxides and other compounds when suitable reagents are added.

TOPIC 04 • TRANSITION-METAL REDOX

Transition Metal Redox Chemistry

Transition metals commonly participate in redox reactions because they can exist in several oxidation states.

Vanadium

Vanadium provides an important example of a metal displaying several oxidation states and differently coloured aqueous ions.

Chromium

Chromium compounds demonstrate changes in oxidation state and colour during redox reactions.

Manganese

Manganese compounds, including permanganate ions, are important oxidising agents.

Exam focus:

Be able to use half-equations and oxidation numbers to analyse redox reactions.

TOPIC 05 • ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

Arenes

Arenes are aromatic hydrocarbons containing one or more benzene rings.

Benzene

Benzene has a planar hexagonal structure with delocalised electrons spread across the ring.

Delocalised π-Electron System
Key idea:

The delocalised π-electron system gives benzene unusual stability.

Electrophilic Substitution

Benzene commonly undergoes electrophilic substitution reactions rather than addition reactions.

Nitration

Benzene reacts with concentrated nitric acid in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid to form nitrobenzene.

Friedel–Crafts Reactions

Friedel–Crafts reactions introduce alkyl or acyl groups into an aromatic ring.

TOPIC 06 • PHENOLS

Phenols

Phenols contain a hydroxyl group directly attached to an aromatic ring.

Acidity

Phenol is more acidic than an alcohol because the phenoxide ion formed after loss of H⁺ is stabilised by delocalisation.

Important Reactions

  • Reaction with sodium.
  • Reaction with sodium hydroxide.
  • Reaction with bromine water.
  • Nitration.
Exam connection:

Compare phenol with alcohols by considering the stability of the conjugate base.

TOPIC 07 • ORGANIC NITROGEN

Amines

Amines are organic compounds containing nitrogen. They can be classified as primary, secondary or tertiary.

Basicity

Amines act as bases because the nitrogen atom contains a lone pair of electrons that can accept a proton.

RNH₂ + H⁺ → RNH₃⁺

TOPIC 08 • AMIDES

Amides

Amides contain the functional group derived from a carboxylic acid in which the hydroxyl group has been replaced by nitrogen.

Formation

Acyl chlorides can react with ammonia or amines to form amides.

Hydrolysis

Amides can undergo hydrolysis under acidic or alkaline conditions.

Structure alert:

Recognise the combination of a carbonyl group and nitrogen when identifying an amide.

TOPIC 09 • AMINO ACIDS

Amino Acids

Amino acids contain both an amino group and a carboxylic acid group.

Zwitterions

In aqueous solution, amino acids can exist as zwitterions containing both a positive and a negative charge.

Important:

Amino acids can act as both acids and bases because they contain acidic and basic functional groups.

Peptide Bonds

Amino acids join together through condensation reactions to form peptide bonds.

TOPIC 10 • PROTEINS

Proteins

Proteins are polymers made from amino acids joined by peptide bonds.

Primary Structure

The primary structure is the sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain.

Secondary Structure

Secondary structure includes structures such as α-helices and β-sheets.

Tertiary Structure

Tertiary structure describes the overall three-dimensional folding of a polypeptide chain.

Hydrogen Bonding

Hydrogen bonds and other intermolecular interactions help maintain protein structure.

Structure hierarchy:

Primary → Secondary → Tertiary

TOPIC 11 • ORGANIC SYNTHESIS

Organic Synthesis

Organic synthesis involves converting one organic compound into another through a sequence of chemical reactions.

Planning a Synthesis

  • Identify the starting material.
  • Identify the target molecule.
  • Choose suitable reagents.
  • Choose appropriate reaction conditions.
  • Consider purification and yield.
Exam focus:

Unit 5 can include synoptic questions requiring knowledge from earlier units.

TOPIC 12 • ORGANIC ANALYSIS

Organic Analysis

Infrared Spectroscopy

Infrared spectroscopy can be used to identify functional groups from characteristic absorption frequencies.

Mass Spectrometry

Mass spectrometry can provide information about molecular mass and fragmentation patterns.

NMR Spectroscopy

Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy provides information about different chemical environments within a molecule.

Simplified Analytical Spectrum

Combine several pieces of analytical evidence rather than relying on one spectrum alone.

EXAM PRACTICE

Chemistry 5 Mixed Practice

1. What is meant by a transition element?
Show answer

An element whose atom has a partially filled d subshell, or which forms at least one ion with a partially filled d subshell.

2. Why are many transition-metal complexes coloured?
Show answer

Electrons can absorb visible radiation and undergo transitions between split d-orbitals. The remaining transmitted or reflected light gives the complex its observed colour.

3. What type of reaction does benzene commonly undergo?
Show answer

Electrophilic substitution.

4. Why are amines basic?
Show answer

The nitrogen atom has a lone pair of electrons that can accept a proton.

5. What is a peptide bond?
Show answer

A bond formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of another amino acid during a condensation reaction.

6. What is the purpose of organic synthesis?
Show answer

To convert an available starting compound into a desired organic product through a suitable sequence of reactions.

7. What is a ligand?
Show answer

A species that donates a lone pair of electrons to a central metal ion to form a coordinate bond.

8. Why is phenol more acidic than an alcohol?
Show answer

The phenoxide ion formed when phenol loses H⁺ is stabilised by delocalisation of the negative charge into the aromatic ring.
