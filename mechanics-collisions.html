MECHANICS 2 • TOPIC 04

Collisions

Learn how momentum and impulse are used to analyse collisions between particles.

LESSON 01

Momentum

Momentum is a vector quantity that describes the motion of a particle.

p = mv

Momentum has both magnitude and direction because velocity is a vector.

LESSON 02

Conservation of Momentum

In a collision where no external resultant force acts on the system, total momentum is conserved.

Total momentum before = Total momentum after
For two particles:

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

LESSON 03

Impulse

Impulse is the change in momentum of a particle.

Impulse = Change in momentum
Impulse = mv − mu

Impulse is measured in newton-seconds (N s).

LESSON 04

Coefficient of Restitution

The coefficient of restitution describes how elastic a collision is.

e = Relative speed of separation / Relative speed of approach
Important:

For ordinary collisions, 0 ≤ e ≤ 1.

LESSON 05

Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

In an elastic collision, kinetic energy is conserved. In an inelastic collision, kinetic energy is not conserved.

  • Momentum is conserved in an isolated system.
  • Kinetic energy may or may not be conserved.
  • The coefficient of restitution helps describe the collision.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Conservation of Momentum

Example

A 2 kg particle moving at 6 m/s collides with a stationary 3 kg particle. After the collision, the 2 kg particle moves at 1 m/s in the same direction. Find the velocity of the 3 kg particle.

Momentum before = 2 × 6 = 12 kg m/s

Momentum after = (2 × 1) + (3v)

12 = 2 + 3v

3v = 10

v = 10/3 m/s

WORKED EXAMPLE

Impulse

Example

A particle of mass 4 kg changes velocity from 3 m/s to 8 m/s in the same direction. Find the impulse.

Impulse = mv − mu

= 4(8) − 4(3)

= 32 − 12

Impulse = 20 N s
