M3 • SECTION 01

Further Kinematics

Study motion when acceleration is not constant and depends on displacement or time.

Lesson 1 — Acceleration as a Function of Time

When acceleration depends on time, we can use integration to find velocity and displacement.

a = dv/dt
v = dx/dt

Integrating acceleration with respect to time gives velocity, while integrating velocity gives displacement.

Lesson 2 — Acceleration as a Function of Displacement

If acceleration is given as a function of displacement, we can use the chain rule.

a = v dv/dx

This allows equations involving acceleration and displacement to be solved without directly using time.

Worked Example

A particle has acceleration a = 6t. Find its velocity if its initial velocity is 4 m/s.

a = dv/dt

dv/dt = 6t

Integrating:

v = 3t² + C

When t = 0, v = 4, so C = 4.

v = 3t² + 4

M3 • SECTION 02

Elastic Strings and Springs

Learn Hooke's law, elastic energy and the mechanics of stretched strings and springs.

Lesson 1 — Hooke's Law

For an elastic string or spring obeying Hooke's law, the tension is proportional to the extension.

T = λx/l

Here λ is the modulus, x is the extension and l is the natural length.

Lesson 2 — Elastic Energy

Work done in stretching an elastic string is stored as elastic potential energy.

E = λx² / 2l

Lesson 3 — Elastic Systems

Problems may involve strings or springs connected to particles and other objects.

  • Find the tension.
  • Find the extension.
  • Apply equilibrium conditions.
  • Use conservation of energy.

Worked Example

An elastic string has modulus 60 N and natural length 2 m. It is extended by 0.5 m. Find its tension.

T = λx/l

T = (60 × 0.5) / 2

T = 15 N

M3 • SECTION 03

Further Dynamics

Explore variable forces, gravitation and simple harmonic motion.

Lesson 1 — Variable Forces

In M3, the applied force may vary with position or time. Newton's second law is still applied.

F = ma

Lesson 2 — Newton's Law of Gravitation

Two masses attract each other with a gravitational force.

F = GMm / r²

Lesson 3 — Simple Harmonic Motion

A particle performs simple harmonic motion when its acceleration is proportional to its displacement from a fixed point and directed towards that point.

d²x/dt² = −ω²x
x = a cos(ωt) or x = a sin(ωt)
v² = ω²(a² − x²)
T = 2π/ω

Lesson 4 — Oscillations of Springs and Strings

Springs and elastic strings can produce oscillatory motion. Problems may involve horizontal or vertical oscillations.

Worked Example

A particle performs SHM with angular speed 4 rad/s. Its amplitude is 0.5 m. Find its period.

T = 2π/ω

T = 2π/4

T = π/2 s

M3 • SECTION 04

Motion in a Circle

Study angular speed, radial acceleration and particles moving in horizontal and vertical circles.

Lesson 1 — Angular Speed

Angular speed measures how quickly an object changes its angular position.

ω = v/r

Lesson 2 — Radial Acceleration

A particle moving in a circle has acceleration directed towards the centre.

a = rω²
a = v²/r

Lesson 3 — Horizontal Circular Motion

Circular motion problems may involve horizontal circles, strings and conical pendulums.

Lesson 4 — Vertical Circular Motion

For vertical circular motion, the forces acting on the particle change relative to its position around the circle.

Both Newton's second law and conservation of energy can be required.

Worked Example

A particle moves in a circle of radius 2 m with speed 6 m/s. Find its radial acceleration.

a = v²/r

a = 6²/2

a = 18 m/s²

M3 • SECTION 05

Statics of Rigid Bodies

Analyse centres of mass and equilibrium of rigid bodies.

Lesson 1 — Centre of Mass

The centre of mass is the point at which the mass of a body may be considered to be concentrated.

M3 includes uniform rigid bodies and simple composite bodies.

Lesson 2 — Equilibrium of Rigid Bodies

A rigid body is in equilibrium when both its resultant force and resultant moment are zero.

Resultant force = 0
Resultant moment = 0

Lesson 3 — Rods, Ladders and Contact Forces

Problems can involve rods or ladders resting against smooth or rough surfaces.

  • Identify all forces.
  • Resolve forces horizontally and vertically.
  • Take moments about a suitable point.
  • Apply equilibrium conditions.

Worked Example

A 20 N force acts 3 m from a pivot. A balancing force acts 5 m from the pivot. Find the balancing force.

20 × 3 = F × 5

60 = 5F

F = 12 N

M3 • COMBINED PRACTICE

M3 Practice Questions

These questions combine concepts from across the M3 syllabus. More questions will be added to this section later.

1. A particle has acceleration a = 4t. Its initial velocity is 3 m/s. Find its velocity at time t.
Show answer

Integrating a = dv/dt gives v = 2t² + 3.

2. An elastic string has natural length 1.5 m, modulus 45 N and extension 0.3 m. Find its tension.
Show answer

T = λx/l

T = (45 × 0.3)/1.5

T = 9 N

3. A particle performs SHM with angular speed 5 rad/s. Find its period.
Show answer

T = 2π/5

T = 2π/5 s

4. A particle moves in a circle of radius 4 m with speed 8 m/s. Find its radial acceleration.
Show answer

a = v²/r

a = 64/4

a = 16 m/s²

5. A force of 15 N acts at a perpendicular distance of 2 m from a pivot. Find its moment.
Show answer

Moment = 15 × 2

Moment = 30 N m
