MECHANICS 2 • TOPIC 02

Centres of Mass

Learn how the centre of mass of particles and systems can be found and used in mechanics problems.

LESSON 01

What is the Centre of Mass?

The centre of mass is the point at which the mass of a system can be considered to be concentrated for many mechanics calculations.

Key idea

For particles arranged along a straight line, the centre of mass depends on both the masses and their positions.

LESSON 02

Two Particles

Consider two particles of masses m₁ and m₂ at positions x₁ and x₂ on a straight line.

x̄ = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂) / (m₁ + m₂)

The formula gives the position of the centre of mass measured from the chosen origin.

LESSON 03

Example with Two Particles

Example

A particle of mass 2 kg is at x = 0 m and a particle of mass 3 kg is at x = 5 m. Find the position of the centre of mass.

x̄ = (2 × 0 + 3 × 5) / (2 + 3)

x̄ = 15 / 5

x̄ = 3 m

LESSON 04

More Than Two Particles

The same principle can be extended to any number of particles.

x̄ = Σ(mx) / Σm

Multiply each mass by its position, add the results, then divide by the total mass.

LESSON 05

Centre of Mass in Two Dimensions

For particles positioned in two dimensions, the centre of mass can be found separately in the horizontal and vertical directions.

x̄ = Σ(mx) / Σm
ȳ = Σ(my) / Σm

This allows the coordinates of the centre of mass to be determined.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Three Particles

Example

Three particles have masses 2 kg, 3 kg and 5 kg at positions 1 m, 4 m and 7 m respectively. Find the centre of mass.

Total mass = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 kg

Σ(mx) = (2 × 1) + (3 × 4) + (5 × 7)

Σ(mx) = 2 + 12 + 35 = 49

x̄ = 49 / 10

x̄ = 4.9 m
