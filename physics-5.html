UNIT 05 • OVERVIEW

Further Physics: From Atoms to the Universe

Unit 5 brings together thermal physics, nuclear physics, oscillations and the physics of stars and the universe. Many of the ideas are connected by energy, motion and fundamental physical laws.

The big picture

Energy transfer → microscopic behaviour → oscillatory motion → stars → galaxies → universe.

TOPIC 01

Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics describes energy transfers involving temperature, internal energy and changes of state.

Internal Energy

The internal energy of a system is the sum of the microscopic kinetic and potential energies of its particles.

ΔE = mcΔθ

where ΔE is the change in internal energy, m is mass, c is specific heat capacity and Δθ is the temperature change.

Specific Latent Heat

During a change of state, energy can be transferred without changing the temperature of the substance.

E = ml

Here l is the specific latent heat.

Ideal Gas Equation

pV = nRT

This relates pressure, volume, amount of gas and absolute temperature for an ideal gas.

WORKED IDEA

A substance of mass 0.50 kg has a specific heat capacity of 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. Its temperature rises by 10 K.

ΔE = mcΔθ

ΔE = 0.50 × 4200 × 10

ΔE = 21 000 J
TOPIC 02

Nuclear Decay

Radioactive decay is a spontaneous and random process in which unstable nuclei transform into more stable configurations.

Activity

A = λN

Activity is the number of nuclear decays per second.

Exponential Decay

N = N₀e−λt

Half-life

T½ = ln(2) / λ
Important

Radioactive decay is random for an individual nucleus, but the behaviour of a very large number of nuclei follows a predictable statistical pattern.

Decay Constant

The decay constant λ represents the probability per unit time that a particular unstable nucleus will decay.

TOPIC 03

Oscillations and Simple Harmonic Motion

Oscillations occur when an object repeatedly moves around an equilibrium position.

Simple Harmonic Motion

In simple harmonic motion, acceleration is proportional to displacement from equilibrium and directed towards equilibrium.

a = −ω²x

Angular Frequency

ω = 2πf

Maximum Speed

vmax = Aω

Energy in SHM

E = ½mω²A²
SHM condition

The acceleration must always act towards the equilibrium position and have magnitude proportional to displacement.

TOPIC 04

Springs and Oscillations

Springs provide a useful physical system for studying oscillatory motion and elastic potential energy.

Hooke's Law

F = kx

The relationship applies while the spring remains within its limit of proportionality.

Elastic Potential Energy

E = ½kx²

Period of a Mass-Spring System

T = 2π√(m/k)
WORKED IDEA

Increasing the mass attached to a spring increases the period of oscillation.

From T = 2π√(m/k), the period is proportional to the square root of the mass.

TOPIC 05

Simple Pendulum

For small oscillations, the period of a simple pendulum depends on its length and the gravitational field strength.

T = 2π√(l/g)

The mass of the pendulum bob does not affect the period in the ideal model.

Experimental connection

A graph of T² against l should produce a straight line through the origin for an ideal simple pendulum.

TOPIC 06

Gravitational Fields and Orbits

Gravitational fields describe the influence of a mass on another mass.

Gravitational Field Strength

g = GM/r²

Gravitational Potential

Vg = −GM/r

Orbital Speed

v = √(GM/r)

The orbital speed of a circular orbit depends on the mass of the central object and the orbital radius.

TOPIC 07

Astrophysics

Astrophysics applies physics to stars, galaxies and other astronomical objects.

Stellar Evolution

A star's evolution depends strongly on its initial mass. Stars spend much of their lives on the main sequence, where nuclear fusion provides the energy that balances gravitational collapse.

  • Nebula
  • Protostar
  • Main sequence
  • Red giant or supergiant
  • Final stellar remnant
Key principle

The greater the initial mass of a star, the more rapidly it generally consumes its nuclear fuel.

TOPIC 08

Luminosity and Intensity

Luminosity is the total power radiated by a star. Intensity describes the power received per unit area.

L = 4πR²σT⁴

This is the Stefan-Boltzmann relationship for a star approximated as a black body.

Intensity

I = L/(4πd²)

As distance increases, the intensity received from a source decreases according to the inverse-square relationship.

TOPIC 09

Stellar Spectra and Temperature

The spectrum of a star contains information about its temperature, composition and motion.

Wien's Law

λmaxT = constant

Hotter stars have shorter peak wavelengths, while cooler stars have longer peak wavelengths.

Spectral Lines

Absorption and emission lines can be used to identify elements present in astronomical objects.

Exam connection

Spectral evidence allows astronomers to determine physical properties of objects that cannot be directly sampled.

TOPIC 10

Cosmology and the Expanding Universe

Cosmology studies the origin, evolution and large-scale structure of the universe.

Redshift

When light from a distant object is shifted towards longer wavelengths, the object is described as redshifted.

z = Δλ/λ

Hubble's Law

v = H₀d

The approximate proportionality between recession velocity and distance provides evidence that the universe is expanding.

Density of the Universe

The average density of the universe is important when considering its large-scale evolution.

Big idea

Observations such as galactic redshift provide evidence for an expanding universe.

Experimental Physics

  • Control relevant variables.

  • Calculate uncertainties.
  • Plot and interpret graphs.

  • Identify anomalous results.
  • Evaluate limitations.
  • Suggest realistic improvements.
Practical reminder
