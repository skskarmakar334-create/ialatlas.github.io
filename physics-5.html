Physics Unit 5

Thermodynamics, radiation, oscillations, astrophysics and cosmology — explained through complete lessons, equations, examples and exam-focused practice.

TOPIC 01

Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics describes the behaviour of matter in terms of temperature, internal energy, pressure and volume.

Internal Energy

The internal energy of a system is the sum of the random kinetic energy and intermolecular potential energy of its particles.

Internal energy:

Internal energy is the total microscopic energy associated with the particles in a system.

Temperature

Temperature is related to the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance.

Specific Heat Capacity

The specific heat capacity of a substance is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of the substance by 1 K.

ΔE = mcΔθ

Specific Latent Heat

Specific latent heat is the energy required to change the state of 1 kg of a substance without changing its temperature.

E = ml

Gas Laws

For a fixed mass of gas, pressure, volume and temperature are related.

pV = nRT

where p is pressure, V is volume, n is amount of substance, R is the molar gas constant and T is absolute temperature.

Pressure and Temperature

For a fixed mass of gas at constant volume, pressure is proportional to absolute temperature.

p ∝ T

TOPIC 02

Nuclear Radiation

Radioactive decay is a spontaneous and random process in which an unstable nucleus changes into a more stable configuration.

Activity

Activity is the number of nuclear decays occurring per unit time.

A = λN

Activity is measured in becquerels (Bq).

Decay Constant

The decay constant λ represents the probability per unit time that a particular nucleus will decay.

Radioactive Decay Equation

N = N₀e−λt

The same exponential behaviour applies to activity:

A = A₀e−λt

Half-Life

Half-life is the time required for the number of undecayed nuclei, or the activity, to fall to half its initial value.

T½ = ln(2) / λ

Types of Radiation

  • Alpha radiation consists of helium nuclei.
  • Beta-minus radiation consists of electrons emitted from the nucleus.
  • Gamma radiation is electromagnetic radiation emitted from an excited nucleus.

Random Nature of Decay

It is impossible to predict exactly when an individual nucleus will decay. However, the behaviour of a large collection of nuclei can be modelled statistically.

TOPIC 03

Oscillations

An oscillation is a repeated motion about an equilibrium position.

Simple Harmonic Motion

Simple harmonic motion occurs when the acceleration is proportional to the displacement from equilibrium and directed towards equilibrium.

a = −ω²x

Angular Frequency

ω = 2πf

Displacement

The displacement of an object undergoing simple harmonic motion can be represented using a sinusoidal function.

x = A cos(ωt)

Velocity

v = −Aω sin(ωt)

Maximum Speed

vmax = Aω

Maximum Acceleration

amax = Aω²

Energy in SHM

During simple harmonic motion, energy transfers between kinetic and potential energy while the total mechanical energy remains constant in an ideal system.

E = ½mω²A²

TOPIC 04

Springs and Oscillating Systems

A mass attached to a spring can oscillate about its equilibrium position.

Hooke's Law

F = kx

provided the limit of proportionality has not been exceeded.

Elastic Potential Energy

E = ½kx²

Mass-Spring System

The period of a mass-spring oscillator depends on the mass and the spring constant.

T = 2π√(m/k)

TOPIC 05

Simple Pendulum

A simple pendulum provides a useful example of approximately simple harmonic motion for small angular displacements.

T = 2π√(l/g)

The period depends on the length of the pendulum and gravitational field strength.

Investigation

The relationship between period and length can be investigated experimentally by measuring the time for several oscillations and repeating the experiment for different lengths.

TOPIC 06

Astrophysics

Astrophysics applies physical principles to stars, galaxies and other astronomical objects.

Gravitational Field Strength

g = GM/r²

Orbital Motion

Objects remain in orbit because gravitational attraction provides the centripetal force required for circular motion.

Orbital Speed

v = √(GM/r)

Orbital Period

T² ∝ r³

Stars

Stars form from clouds of gas and dust. Gravitational collapse raises the temperature and pressure until nuclear fusion can occur.

The eventual evolution of a star depends strongly on its initial mass.

TOPIC 07

Stellar Evolution

Main Sequence

During the main-sequence stage, stars produce energy through nuclear fusion in their cores.

Low and Medium Mass Stars

After leaving the main sequence, these stars can expand into red giants and eventually form white dwarfs.

Massive Stars

Massive stars can develop into supergiants and undergo supernova explosions. Their remnants may become neutron stars or black holes.

Key idea:

Stellar evolution is strongly determined by the initial mass of the star.

TOPIC 08

Luminosity and Stellar Measurements

Luminosity

Luminosity is the total power emitted by a star.

L = 4πr²σT⁴

This is the Stefan-Boltzmann law.

Inverse Square Law

I = L / (4πd²)

The intensity received from a source decreases with the square of the distance from the source.

Wien's Law

λmaxT = constant

Hotter stars have their peak emission at shorter wavelengths.

TOPIC 09

Stellar Spectra

Spectroscopy allows scientists to determine information about stars from the light they emit or absorb.

Absorption Lines

Dark absorption lines occur when atoms in a cooler gas absorb particular wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation.

Emission Lines

Bright emission lines are produced when excited atoms emit photons at specific wavelengths.

Doppler Effect

The wavelength of light received from an astronomical object changes if the object is moving relative to the observer.

Δλ / λ = v / c

This relationship can be used to estimate radial velocity when the speed is much smaller than the speed of light.

TOPIC 10

Cosmology

Cosmology investigates the origin, structure and evolution of the Universe.

Expansion of the Universe

Observations of distant galaxies show that the Universe is expanding.

Hubble's Law

v = H₀d

The recession velocity of a galaxy is approximately proportional to its distance from Earth.

Big Bang Model

The Big Bang model describes a Universe that began in an extremely hot and dense state and has expanded and cooled over time.

Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation

The cosmic microwave background is electromagnetic radiation originating from the early Universe. Its existence provides important evidence supporting the Big Bang model.

TOPIC 11

Redshift and the Expanding Universe

When light from a distant galaxy is shifted towards longer wavelengths, it is described as redshifted.

z = Δλ / λ

For relatively small velocities:

z ≈ v/c

Measurements of redshift provide evidence that distant galaxies are generally receding from us.

TOPIC 12

Density and the Fate of the Universe

The large-scale behaviour of the Universe depends on the balance between its expansion and gravitational effects.

Critical Density

Critical density is the density associated with a particular boundary between different possible large-scale geometries and expansion behaviours.

Dark Matter and Dark Energy

Observations of galaxies and cosmic expansion indicate that ordinary matter does not account for all of the gravitational and expansion behaviour observed in the Universe.

Dark matter is used to explain additional gravitational effects, while dark energy is associated with the observed accelerated expansion of the Universe.

Unit 5 Practical Investigations

Unit 5 also develops practical and mathematical skills. Experimental work should involve planning, measurements, analysis, uncertainties and evaluation.

Possible Investigations

  • Investigating the relationship between volume and temperature of a fixed mass of gas.

PRACTICE

Unit 5 Mixed Practice

1. A 2.0 kg mass is heated through 15 K. Its specific heat capacity is 900 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. Calculate the energy transferred.
Show answer

ΔE = mcΔθ

ΔE = 2.0 × 900 × 15 = 27 000 J

2. What is meant by half-life?
Show answer

Half-life is the time required for the number of undecayed nuclei, or the activity, to fall to half its initial value.

3. A radioactive isotope has a decay constant of 0.20 s⁻¹. Calculate its half-life.
Show answer

T½ = ln(2) / λ

T½ = 0.693 / 0.20 = 3.47 s

4. State the condition required for simple harmonic motion.
Show answer

The acceleration must be proportional to the displacement from equilibrium and directed towards the equilibrium position.

5. A spring has a spring constant of 200 N m⁻¹ and is stretched by 0.10 m. Calculate its elastic potential energy.
Show answer

E = ½kx²

E = ½ × 200 × (0.10)² = 1.0 J

6. What provides the centripetal force for a planet orbiting a star?
Show answer

The gravitational attraction between the planet and the star provides the centripetal force.

7. State Hubble's law.
Show answer

The recession velocity of a distant galaxy is approximately proportional to its distance from Earth: v = H₀d.

8. Explain why redshift provides evidence for an expanding Universe.
Show answer

Light from distant galaxies is observed to have wavelengths shifted towards the red end of the spectrum. This indicates that the galaxies are generally moving away from us. The relationship between recession velocity and distance supports the conclusion that the Universe is expanding.
