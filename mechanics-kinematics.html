Kinematics in a Straight Line

Learn how displacement, velocity and acceleration describe the motion of particles.

LESSON 01

What is Kinematics?

Kinematics is the study of motion without considering the forces that cause the motion.

Key idea

Kinematics describes how an object moves.

In Mechanics 1, we mainly study the motion of a particle along a straight line.

LESSON 02

Displacement

Displacement is the directed distance of a particle from a chosen origin.

Displacement is a vector quantity.

Therefore, it has both magnitude and direction.

Displacement is commonly represented by the variable s.

LESSON 03

Velocity

Velocity describes the rate of change of displacement with respect to time.

v = ds/dt

Velocity is a vector quantity, so its sign indicates direction along the chosen line.

LESSON 04

Acceleration

Acceleration describes the rate of change of velocity with respect to time.

a = dv/dt

Acceleration is also a vector quantity.

LESSON 05

Constant Acceleration

When acceleration is constant, we can use the standard constant-acceleration equations.

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t

Here:

  • u = initial velocity
  • v = final velocity
  • a = constant acceleration
  • t = time
  • s = displacement

WORKED EXAMPLE

Using a Constant-Acceleration Equation

Example

A particle starts with velocity 5 m/s and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 4 seconds. Find its final velocity.

Use:

v = u + at

u = 5, a = 2, t = 4

v = 5 + (2)(4)

v = 13 m/s

LESSON 06

Speed and Velocity

Speed tells us how fast a particle is moving. Velocity also tells us the direction of motion.

Speed

Magnitude of velocity.

A particle can have constant speed while its velocity changes if its direction changes.
